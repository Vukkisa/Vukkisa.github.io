The five questions

01What problem was I trying to solve?

Traffic rules in India live in dense legal documents. People ask forwarded messages and search engines instead, and get confident, half-remembered answers. I wanted something that answers from the official text and can show where each answer came from.

02What did I build?

A RAG assistant. Official government documents are cleaned, chunked and embedded (nomic-embed-text via Ollama) into Chroma. A LangChain LCEL chain retrieves context and hands it to a Groq-hosted LLM. FastAPI serves it; a plain HTML/JavaScript frontend sits on top.

03What was technically difficult?

Not the happy path — the refusal path. Getting a model to say "I can't answer that from these documents" reliably, instead of helpfully inventing something. And the data: 400+ documents isn't a knowledge base until you decide what stays.

04What did I learn?

Retrieval quality is mostly decided before the model ever runs — in extraction, cleaning and chunking. And a good "no" is a feature you have to design and test, not a side effect.

05What would I improve?

A proper evaluation set (questions with known answers and known refusals), hybrid search with a reranker, page-level citations, and tracking retrieval hit-rate over time.

    Walk the pipeline

    The five questions

    01What problem was I trying to solve?

    Spaces that should be empty sometimes aren't, and nobody watches a camera feed all day. I wanted a system that watches for me and speaks up only when a person is somewhere they shouldn't be.

    02What did I build?

    A real-time pipeline: YOLOv8 + OpenCV over a webcam, video file or RTSP stream; zones that can be redefined on the fly; event logging with snapshots and clips saved as evidence. CUDA-optimised to roughly 35 FPS, about 24 ms per frame.

    03What was technically difficult?

    Latency, not accuracy. Keeping frame capture, inference and logging from blocking each other — and stopping one person standing still from producing a hundred events.

    04What did I learn?

    The model is a small part of a vision system. The rest is I/O, threading, thresholds and deciding what counts as an "event".

    05What would I improve?

    Tracking IDs across frames, person re-identification so "unauthorised" can mean a specific someone, a small dashboard for reviewing events, and a lighter build for edge devices.

    -- 07 / experiments & playground

    Small experiments.
    Some of them are even right.

    Each one is a tiny working model on synthetic or hand-made data. Poke it. Break it. That's what the “broke” line is for.

    EXP—01 · time seriesworks (mostly)

    Forecast shootout

    Four ways to predict the next 12 months. Train on 36, hide 12. Toggle models, then re-roll the noise and watch the winner change.

    tried · broke · learned
    tried
    naive, seasonal-naive, linear trend, trend + seasonality.
    broke
    my "clever" model lost to copy last year more often than I'd like.
    learned
    always beat the dumb baseline before you earn the right to be fancy.
    EXP—02 · recommenderhalf-works

    Taste engine

    Pick one to three films you like. Content-based cosine similarity does the rest — and tells you why.

      tried · broke · learned
      tried
      hand-tagged feature vectors, cosine similarity to a summed profile.
      broke
      cold start: with one like, everything recommended is a near-clone.
      learned
      "because you liked X" is half the product. Explanations build trust.
      EXP—03 · NLPhumbling

      Which words moved the needle?

      A tiny sentiment model with a hand-written lexicon and one rule: “not” flips the next word. Type anything.

      tried · broke · learned
      tried
      bag of weighted words, plus negation.
      broke
      sarcasm, obviously. "Oh great, another bug" scores as positive.
      learned
      why context-aware models exist — and why I should still look at the errors.
      EXP—04 · visionfragile

      Pixel brain

      Draw a digit on a 5×7 grid. A nearest-template classifier guesses. Try drawing it badly.

      ?

        tried · broke · learned
        tried
        Jaccard overlap against ten template digits.
        broke
        shift a stroke by one cell and it changes its mind.
        learned
        that's the problem convolutions solve. I felt it before I read it.
        EXP—05 · agentsscripted replay

        The agent loop, slowed down

        Thought → action → observation, replayed step by step for two goals. One is possible. One isn't. The second is the interesting one.

          tried · broke · learned
          tried
          a tool-using loop with a step budget.
          broke
          it wanted to be helpful with tools it didn't have.
          learned
          knowing what you can't do is the whole safety story. Same lesson as the RAG refusal.
